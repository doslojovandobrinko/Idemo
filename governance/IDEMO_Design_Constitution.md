# IDEMO DESIGN CONSTITUTION
**Level 2: The Governing Design Standard**

---

## 1. Scope & Authority
The **IDEMO Design Constitution** establishes the visual language, typographic constants, touch ergonomics, accessibility requirements, and layout standards for the IDEMO ecosystem.
* It is subordinate exclusively to the **IDEMO Constitution** (Highest Governing Document).
* It represents the absolute standard for preventing visual regression and preserving the premium, editorial feel of the application.

---

## 2. IDEMO Editorial Luxury Design Language
IDEMO does not subscribe to or borrow from external, passing design trends. Instead, it is defined by its own proprietary **IDEMO Editorial Luxury Design Language**, which is characterized by:
* **Calm:** Minimizing cognitive load and visual tension through balanced proportions.
* **Editorial:** Pairing high-end typography and structured layouts to present travel experiences like a prestigious collector's volume.
* **Timeless:** Avoiding trendy, flashy, or glossy elements that age quickly, ensuring multi-year visual durability.
* **Premium:** Conveying prestige through restraint and perfect alignment rather than unnecessary ornamentation.
* **Understated:** Relying on subtle contrasts, refined borders, and gentle shadows instead of loud attention-seeking elements.
* **Tactile:** Interactive elements feel physical, responding to touches and hovers with highly organic micro-animations.
* **Elegant:** A sophisticated neutral palette highlighted by selective, purposeful color accents.
* **Human-Centered:** Placing the traveler's immediate comfort, safety, and readability first.
* **Highly Readable:** Ensuring perfect contrast and sizing for quick glance reading under any environment.
* **Privacy-First:** The visual interface never compromises or tracks user data; all visual state transitions remain sovereign to the user's device.
* **Minimal Without Feeling Empty:** Utilizing negative space as a deliberate luxury feature to give content room to breathe, while maintaining functional density where required.

## 3. Design Principles (0 – 18)

### Principle 0 — Preservation Before Improvement
The current IDEMO interface is the baseline reference implementation. Assume all existing layouts, spacing, typography, colors, animations, shadows, and interaction patterns are intentional and validated. Modify an element ONLY if it produces a measurable improvement in readability, accessibility, usability, consistency, performance, or platform compliance.

### Principle 1 — Readability First
Every screen must remain comfortable to use on a standard 6.5-inch mobile display under bright daylight, outdoor conditions, one-handed operation, or quick glance interactions. Users should never need to zoom, squint, or increase system font size to read important information. Premium aesthetics must never reduce readability.

### Principle 2 — Luxury Through Restraint
IDEMO is luxurious because it is calm, balanced, tactile, understated, refined, confident, and intentional. Luxury must never be achieved through unnecessary decoration, glowing borders, or flashy gradients. Visual noise is strictly prohibited.

### Principle 3 — Immediate Comprehension
Every screen should answer three questions within three seconds:
1. *Where am I?*
2. *What is important?*
3. *What should I do next?*
Users should recognize options rather than interpret complex structures.

### Principle 4 — Functional Hierarchy
Visual attention must naturally flow: **Headline → Primary Content → Supporting Information → Decorative Details**. Decorative elements must never compete with or distract from functional information.

### Principle 5 — Typography
Typography must prioritize readability over artistic expression. 
* **Editorial Serifs (e.g., Space Grotesk, Outfit, Playfair):** Reserved primarily for headings, titles, feature names, and storytelling.
* **Highly Legible Sans-Serifs (e.g., Inter, JetBrains Mono):** Reserved for core interface controls, descriptions, numbers, and metadata.
* Avoid excessive font variations. Prefer no more than three distinct text styles within a single card.

### Principle 6 — Contrast
Maintain an elegant neutral palette while ensuring excellent readability. Avoid low-contrast color combinations. Important information must immediately stand out.

### Principle 7 — White Space
White space exists to improve cognitive focus and comprehension. Never add empty space simply because space is available. Compact layouts are preferred when readability is preserved. Every millimeter of spacing should have a clear purpose.

### Principle 8 — Card Philosophy
Cards should feel like premium physical objects with subtle elevation, refined borders, clear separation, balanced padding, and a tactile appearance. They should feel elegant and light rather than heavy and bulky.

### Principle 9 — Touch Interaction
Every interactive element must clearly communicate: *"This can be pressed."* Buttons must feel tactile, responsive, comfortable, and premium. Touch targets must always meet or exceed minimum accessibility standards (at least 44px).

### Principle 10 — Accessibility
Accessibility is a core aspect of premium quality. All refinements must improve or preserve readability, color contrast, touch targets, and navigation clarity without reducing visual elegance.

### Principle 11 — Consistency
Every screen must immediately feel like IDEMO. Spacing, typography, colors, corner radius, shadows, buttons, cards, icons, and animations must be uniform across the entire application.

### Principle 12 — No Visual Regression
No refinement may reduce readability, clarity, accessibility, discoverability, performance, consistency, or premium appearance. Every accepted change must objectively improve or preserve the current standard.

### Principle 13 — Progressive Disclosure
Display only the information users need at that specific moment. Advanced or secondary information should appear progressively to avoid overwhelming the interface.

### Principle 14 — Premium Motion
Animations must communicate high quality. They must be smooth, subtle, short, purposeful, and consistent. Motion should support the experience, never distract from it.

### Principle 15 — Cognitive Simplicity
Reduce thinking, interpretation, and ambiguity. Complexity belongs inside the software, never inside the user interface.

### Principle 16 — Performance
Visual refinement must never reduce responsiveness. Scrolling, animations, transitions, and button responses must remain immediate, fluid, and run at a stable high frame rate.

### Principle 17 — Benchmark Screen
The **Welcome Card** establishes the benchmark for readability, contrast, visual hierarchy, spacing, touch ergonomics, and premium presentation. Every future screen must strive to match this standard.

### Principle 18 — One-Screen Confidence
Whenever reasonably possible, each primary screen must communicate its purpose without requiring scrolling. The first viewport must establish context, primary information, and the primary action.

## 4. Governing Design Standard Amendments

### Principle 19 — Baseline Reference Implementation
The current IDEMO application is the Baseline Reference Implementation. It represents the accumulated result of extensive refinement and validation. It shall be preserved unless a proposed change demonstrates a clear, objective, and measurable improvement without introducing visual, functional, or architectural regression.

### Principle 20 — Mandatory Design Impact Review
Before modifying any existing interface, perform a Design Impact Review documenting:
* Purpose of the requested change.
* Components affected and expected user benefit.
* Potential regression risks, impact on consistency, accessibility, readability, performance, and premium editorial identity.
* *If risks exceed benefits, preserve the current implementation.*

### Principle 21 — Component-First Evolution
IDEMO is a unified Design System, not a collection of independent screens. Every visual element should originate from reusable components. Avoid duplicate visual solutions, parallel component implementations, or isolated styling.

### Principle 22 — Measured Evolution
Every refinement should represent the smallest possible change necessary to achieve the objective. Avoid large redesigns when a targeted improvement will achieve the same result.

### Principle 23 — Stability Over Novelty
New visual trends, fashionable effects, and contemporary UI patterns shall not be adopted solely because they are popular. Every addition must support IDEMO’s established identity of calm, elegant, editorial, tactile, understated, timeless, and highly readable design.

### Principle 24 — Whole-Application Thinking
No screen shall be modified in isolation. Evaluate all changes against the entire application to ensure visual, interaction, spacing, typographic, and navigation consistency.

### Principle 25 — Evidence-Based Decisions
Design decisions shall be based on objective criteria rather than subjective preference. Justification requires measurable improvements in readability, accessibility, discoverability, usability, performance, compliance, or defect correction.

### Principle 26 — Operational Commitment Principle
Visitors may freely discover, compare, save, and organise curated experiences without entering an operational workflow. Inquiry creation, consent collection, and Partner Portal routing begin only after the visitor explicitly confirms an arrangement request from My Event Planner.

## 5. Mandatory Regression Protection Checklist
Before applying any visual or UI change, verify the following:
* [ ] Existing visual identity preserved
* [ ] No visual regression introduced
* [ ] Readability maintained or improved (outdoor/daylight test passed)
* [ ] Accessibility maintained or improved (min 44px touch targets)
* [ ] Premium appearance preserved
* [ ] Consistency with existing screens maintained
* [ ] Smallest necessary change rule applied
* [ ] Performance remains fluid and unaffected
* [ ] Responsive behavior fully verified on desktop and mobile
